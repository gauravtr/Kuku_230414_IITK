- **Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar** — Supreme Court · 2014 · (2014) 8 SCC 273
  - Why it fits your case Applies when a dowry-cruelty police case, and possible arrest, is in the picture. You said: My wife's family has filed a dowry FIR against me and my parents. Police…
  - Held: Police must not arrest automatically in dowry-cruelty cases (offences punishable up to 7 years). They must record why arrest is necessary, and magistrates must check this before ordering detention. The guidelines continue under the new criminal codes.
  - What it means for you: Protects your elderly parents from automatic arrest by requiring the police to record specific reasons.
  - It limits arrest; it does not stop the case, the investigation or a DV Act application.
  - Source: https://legalfly.in/case-summary-arnesh-kumar-v-state-of-bihar/

**Against you: what the other side may cite**

- **Rajnesh v. Neha** — Supreme Court · 2020 · (2021) 2 SCC 324
  - Why it fits your case Applies when money for her own or the children's upkeep (maintenance) is in issue. You said: …tomorrow at 11 am. My wife has also filed for maintenance in the family court.
  - Held: Laid down binding guidelines for maintenance: both sides must file an affidavit disclosing income, assets and liabilities; maintenance runs from the date the application is filed; courts must decide interim maintenance quickly. The Supreme Court has since ordered this judgment re-circulated to all judges.
  - The disclosure affidavit applies to her too: she must also disclose her own income and assets.
  - Source: https://www.mondaq.com/supreme-court-clarifies-the-law-on-maintenance/1034570
- **Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja** — Supreme Court · 2020 · 2020 INSC 599; [2020] 12 SCR 189 (3 judges)
  - Why it fits your case Applies when she lived in the in-laws' house after marriage and is being kept out of it. From your case file: Wife left the matrimonial home after arguments; sent WhatsApp message…
  - Held: A 'shared household' is not limited to a house owned or rented by the husband. A wife can claim the right to live in the in-laws' house where she lived after marriage, even if her father-in-law owns it. This overruled the older narrow view in S.R. Batra.
  - The right to residence is protected, but it is not an ownership right; the court balances it case by case.
  - Source: https://www.scobserver.in/journal/court-reinterprets-shared-household-under-the-domestic-violence-law/
